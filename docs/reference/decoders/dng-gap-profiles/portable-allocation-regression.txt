# Portable allocation regression

This artifact compares the removed Linux huge-page output allocation policy
with plain zeroed `Vec` allocation. It is a host-specific observation, not a
portable performance guarantee.

- Host: WSL2 Debian, AMD Ryzen 7 5800H, 15 visible CPUs.
- Kernel: `6.6.87.1-microsoft-standard-WSL2`.
- glibc: `2.41-12+deb13u3`.
- THP: enabled `[madvise]`; defrag `[madvise]`.
- Build: release, page-cache-hot, single-thread and 15-thread cases.
- Timings: three fresh interleaved processes per decoder, five measured rounds
  after one warmup in each process. Values are medians of the 15 rounds.
- Faults: one separate page-cache-hot decode after warmup, measured with
  `perf stat -e minor-faults,page-faults,instructions`.
- Equality: the three-way dimensions, storage type, bits, and pixel gate passed
  for all seven files on the final portable state.

Fornax instruction totals moved by less than 0.01% for every case. The
regression is first-touch/fault cost, not added scalar work.

## Parity conclusion

Without the Linux policy:

- 5D3 uncompressed loses parity in both thread modes. The one-thread ratio moves
  from approximately parity to 2.75x FlashRaw.
- FP16 and FP32 Deflate lose their previous parity/lead and are slower than
  FlashRaw in both thread modes.
- Pixel 8 LJPEG moves from 1.91x to 2.21x FlashRaw at one thread and from 1.73x
  to 1.95x at 15 threads.
- 5D3 LJPEG remains compute-bound and slower, but also loses an additional
  16.8% at one thread.
- Sigma fp remains faster than FlashRaw at one thread and effectively at
  parity at 15 threads. Its wall-time changes are inside noise despite a lower
  fault count with the policy.
- iPhone SE wall time is compute-dominated; the fault change does not establish
  a material policy win.

The corrected source measurements therefore remain useful evidence about THP,
but the policy is not portable and is removed from the codec.
